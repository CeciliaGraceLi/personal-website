Thoughts on Analytics — Hard or Soft skills

Before my conversations with professionals in the analytics field, I always believed that analytics was primarily a hard skill that required technical knowledge and expertise. However, after speaking with several professionals, I realized that soft skills are just as important in the field of analytics.

When thinking about analytics, the first skill that comes to mind is "analytical thinking," which I interpret as thinking logically and thoroughly. By considering different factors and weighing their importance, we use data to understand trends and predict future performance.

At first, it seemed like there was little room for soft skills. But as I talked with people working in business analytics, people analytics, and financial analytics, the first skills they mentioned when I asked what was most important were never SQL, Python, or JavaScript. Instead, they mentioned "curiosity," "approachability," and "teamwork."

It is fascinating that almost every kind of job, even those that are heavily technology-focused, relies on how you interact and cooperate with others. "Be the person you want to work with." I remember one of my mentors mentioning this when we were on a retreat in New Hampshire.

These conversations broadened my understanding of analytics and reinforced my interest in developing myself more holistically. Technical skills may help you do the work, but being someone that people want to work with can make just as much of a difference.
