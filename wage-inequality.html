Econometrics

Wage Inequality Econometric Analysis

STATA Regression Analysis

Problem

Do wage gaps persist between demographic groups once you control for the usual explanations — education, experience, and industry? I wanted to test how much of the observed wage gap is explained by these factors, and how much remains unexplained.

Dataset

75K+ labor-market observations, including hourly and annual wage, education level, years of experience, industry, and demographic variables.

Methodology

I used multivariate OLS (ordinary least squares) regression in STATA, with wage as the dependent variable and education, experience, and industry as controls, to isolate the portion of the wage gap not explained by these factors.

  • Cleaned and prepared the dataset, handling missing values and outliers
  • Checked for multicollinearity between predictor variables
  • Ran multivariate OLS regression with robust standard errors
  • Validated model assumptions (linearity, homoscedasticity, normality of residuals)

Lessons Learned & What I'd Improve

  • Cross-sectional data can only show correlation at a point in time — I'd incorporate panel data to track the same individuals over time and get closer to a causal estimate.
  • Robust standard errors mattered more than I expected once I checked for heteroscedasticity — a reminder to validate assumptions before trusting p-values.
  • Next time, I'd test interaction effects between education and industry rather than treating them as independent controls.
